Gifted screening measures two different things.

Almost every gifted pathway looks at ability and achievement separately. They are not the same test, they do not respond to the same preparation, and most practice apps only touch one of them.

Half one · ability

Reasoning

Cognitive and ability batteries measure reasoning with deliberately unfamiliar material: figure matrices, verbal analogies, number series, paper folding. Practice does not raise a true ability score. What it removes is the shock of seeing a format for the first time under timed conditions, which is a real and separate disadvantage for a six-year-old who has never met one.

Half two · achievement

What a child knows

Achievement batteries measure grade-level reading and mathematics against published standards. This half genuinely moves with daily practice, and on most eligibility routes it is a criterion in its own right. It is the honest target of any study program, and it is where the majority of this app’s ten thousand questions sit.

The position we hold

No app can guarantee a gifted placement, and any app that says otherwise is selling you something. The Gifted League trains achievement, which moves, and rehearses ability formats, which builds familiarity and calm rather than raw reasoning power. Everything on this site is written to that line.

Ability formats

The Mind Gym: six item types, generated fresh.

These are the formats cognitive batteries are built from. Nothing here is a scanned worksheet; every puzzle is constructed at the moment it appears, scaled to the child’s grade, with distractors chosen to be the mistakes children actually make.

Figure matrices

Find the rule

Two pictures change by a hidden rule; the child applies the same rule to a second pair. Single transformations at kindergarten, rotation added at second grade, two rules composed at fourth and fifth.

Classification

Odd one out

Four figures, one rule. The three that belong deliberately vary on irrelevant features, balanced so that only the rule isolates the answer and shortcut guessing fails.

Serial reasoning

Figure series

A sequence that progresses by count, shading, size, rotation or shape cycle, and by two attributes at once for older children.

Spatial visualization

Paper folding

Paper is folded once or twice, punched, and opened. The child predicts the hole pattern. One fold to third grade, two folds from fourth.

Verbal reasoning

Analogies

Relational pairs banded by grade: concrete relations early, abstract and opposite relations by fifth grade. Read aloud, so a pre-reader is not blocked by decoding.

Quantitative reasoning

Number patterns

Series that climb from counting on, through skip counting and two-step rules, to squares and triangular numbers.

The daily loop

What ten minutes actually looks like.

Three missions

The app sets three worlds for the day. Two questions in every five reach a step above the child’s ladder, so the road to the reward is the demanding part.

The Game Reward

Finishing the day’s missions plus fifty questions answered and thirty correct unlocks the Grand Prix. The game is earned with work and never with money, which is what keeps a child asking to train.

The daily chest and the streak

Show up, keep the flame alive, open the chest. A stretch break arrives every third round and a wind-down screen after about twenty-five minutes, because two short sessions beat one long one at this age.

Privacy

There is nothing to leak.

The Gifted League is a single file that runs entirely in the browser. It makes no network requests of any kind. There is no account, no analytics, no telemetry and no advertising. Every score, card and history entry is stored only in the browser on the device in front of you, and clearing that site’s data erases it.

This is why the counters in the Grown-ups tab exist. They are the honest substitute for analytics: everything a product team would want to know, computed on the device and shown to the only people entitled to it.

Being precise

How eligibility actually works, using Georgia as the worked example.

Georgia State Board Rule 160-4-2-.38 sets two routes. On the psychometric route a child must reach the 99th percentile on a norm-referenced test of mental ability. Norm-referenced means the score comes from comparing your child against a national sample rather than against a fixed pass mark. For kindergarten through second grade that bar is the 99th percentile and not the 96th, and it must be paired with one qualifying achievement score.

On the multiple-criteria route a child must meet the bar in three of four areas: mental ability at the 96th percentile, achievement at the 90th on the total battery or on total reading or total mathematics, creativity at the 90th, and motivation at the 90th.

Read those two routes carefully and the honest target becomes obvious. Practice moves achievement. It does not move a mental-ability score. The achievement criterion at the 90th percentile appears on both routes and is reachable with the work, and that is what this program aims at. The 99th percentile figure quoted around gifted admission is the mental-ability cutoff, which is a different instrument measuring something practice does not train.

Other states and districts word it differently, but nearly all of them separate ability from achievement in the same way, so the same reasoning carries over. A district screener can also begin a referral, and a referral can come from a teacher or from you.
